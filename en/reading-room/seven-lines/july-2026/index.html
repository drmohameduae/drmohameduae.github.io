Attention Is Strategy

6 July 2026

The most valuable resource in any organization is not capital. It is attention.

Every leader has the same 24 hours. Every institution has finite leadership attention.

Yet many organizations unintentionally spend that attention on urgent issues rather than important ones—chasing emails, resolving avoidable problems, and reacting to the crisis of the day.

Attention is a strategic resource.

Where leaders direct it determines what the organization improves, what it neglects, and ultimately what it becomes.

The highest-performing institutions are intentional about this. They protect leadership time for the decisions that shape the future:

Building capability rather than fixing recurring problems. Strengthening systems rather than relying on heroics. Developing people rather than managing every task. Looking beyond the next quarter to the next decade.

Operational excellence matters, but leaders create the greatest value when they spend more time designing the institution than merely operating it.

A useful question for every executive is:

If someone reviewed my calendar over the past three months, would it reveal that I am managing today's work—or building tomorrow's institution?

Leadership is reflected not only in the decisions we make, but also in where we choose to place our attention.

Ask Better Questions

13 July 2026

The future will not be led by the organizations with the most data. It will be led by those that ask the best questions.

We often hear that data is the new currency.

Yet data, on its own, has never made a strategic decision.

The real differentiator is the quality of the questions leaders ask.

Good questions challenge assumptions. They uncover risks before they become crises. They reveal opportunities others fail to see. And they encourage cultures where learning matters more than certainty.

As artificial intelligence becomes more capable, access to information will become increasingly equal.

What will remain unequal is judgment.

The leaders and institutions that will excel are those that combine data with curiosity, technology with wisdom, and analytics with sound governance.

The question is no longer, "Do we have enough information?"

It is, "Are we asking the questions that truly matter?"

Because the quality of our questions ultimately shapes the quality of our decisions.

And the quality of our decisions shapes the future of our institutions.

Better Decisions at Speed

20 July 2026

In the coming decade, competitive advantage will belong to institutions that can make high-quality decisions faster—not simply those that move faster.

Speed alone is not a strategy.

Making quick decisions with poor judgment only accelerates the wrong outcome.

The real challenge for leaders is reducing the time between insight and sound decision-making without compromising governance, accountability, or strategic thinking.

This is where institutional capability matters.

Organizations that excel are building:

Clear decision rights. Trusted data and intelligence. Simple governance frameworks. Empowered leaders at every level. A culture that values evidence over hierarchy.

Artificial intelligence will make information more accessible than ever.

But it will not replace judgment, integrity, or accountability.

These remain uniquely human responsibilities.

The institutions that will define the future are those that combine technology with disciplined leadership—where decisions are not only faster, but consistently wiser.

In an age of acceleration, the true competitive advantage is not speed.

It is the ability to make better decisions at speed.

What do you believe is the biggest barrier to better decision-making in organizations today: data, culture, governance, or leadership?

July 2026 · 04

Institutional Intelligence

27 July 2026

The next competitive advantage will not come from technology alone. It will come from institutional intelligence.

Artificial intelligence is transforming how we analyze data, automate processes, and generate insights. Yet as these capabilities become widely available, technology itself will become less of a differentiator.

What will distinguish leading organizations is their ability to convert information into institutional intelligence.

Institutional intelligence is the collective capacity to:

Connect data across functions rather than operate in silos. Learn from every decision, success, and failure. Anticipate emerging risks before they become crises. Turn insights into timely, measurable action.

This requires more than digital transformation. It demands a transformation in governance, culture, and leadership.

The institutions that will lead the next decade will not necessarily be those with the most advanced AI platforms. They will be those that combine technology with sound judgment, trusted data, clear accountability, and a commitment to continuous learning.

Technology can accelerate decisions.

Institutional intelligence ensures those decisions are the right ones.

As AI becomes increasingly accessible, perhaps the defining question for leaders is no longer, "How intelligent is our technology?"

It is, "How intelligent is our institution?"
